# Device Checks

Two small pages for finding out how a phone behaves, before anything is built on the answer.

- **Playback** — seven questions about video: whether it starts on its own, whether a scene
  hidden behind another keeps playing, whether a looping clip comes round by itself, how many
  play at once, and whether a still picture stands in when a clip is late.
- **Mirroring** — six questions about right-to-left: whether gestures, arrows and a progress
  line flip with the language while pictures stay as they are.

Each page writes its own answers on screen and offers them as text to copy.

## Before running the playback page

**Set Auto-Lock to Never.** Settings, Display & Brightness, Auto-Lock. It takes a few minutes,
and a phone that locks its screen pauses every video on the page. The page asks to hold the
screen awake by itself and warns you if it cannot — but a notification can still take that hold
away, so the setting is the reliable one.

Leave the page alone while it runs. If it is covered at any point it says so, on screen and in
the copied results, because a verdict decided while the videos were paused is not a verdict.

## Reading the results

Green **Works** and red **No** are findings. Amber **Not tested** is not: it means a clip did not
arrive in time, or the thing being checked never happened. **Five of the seven playback rows can
say it**, and all of the mirroring rows are decided straight away.

The copied text carries what the phone is, how big its screen is, every verdict with its detail
line, anything that failed to load, and all the numbers the page measured. Send that rather than
typing out the verdicts — the numbers are what the findings rest on.

## Worth doing twice

Once normally, and once with **Low Power Mode** switched on. That is the one thing no desktop
browser can answer, and it is the case where iOS refuses to start video at all.
